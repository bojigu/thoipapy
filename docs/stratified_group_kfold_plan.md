# Plan: replace the blind test set with stratified, grouped, repeated k-fold cross-validation

Status: proposal, not yet implemented. Written against commit `d5a8a08`.

Every number quoted here is regenerated by `scripts/stratified_group_kfold_measurements.py`, whose printed sections are numbered to match the sections below.

## 1. Proposal, and what it does and does not buy

Retire the `set08` train / `set07` blind-test division. Evaluate on a single 50-protein pool (`set05`) using repeated `StratifiedGroupKFold`, grouped by homology cluster and stratified by experimental database, with every supervised step (duplicate-feature removal, MDI ranking, ANOVA `SelectKBest`, ensemble RFE, hyperparameter search) moved inside the training fold.

**What this buys.** Two things, neither of which is a tighter error bar on the headline number.

1. **It removes about 0.033 AUC of optimism** introduced by splitting at the residue level, and a further 0.018 from ignoring homology (section 2.4). The current numbers are not wrong by a rounding error; they are wrong by more than the size of the effects the project is trying to measure.
2. **It makes paired comparison possible.** Two configurations evaluated on byte-identical folds compare as paired samples, and the bootstrap interval on the *difference* is 0.023 wide against 0.125 for either arm's absolute interval (section 2.5). That is the instrument that can detect a feature change; the absolute number cannot.

**What it does not buy.** The marginal 95% interval on the pooled out-of-fold AUC is about 0.125 wide, against 0.223 for the blind test. That is a 1.8x narrowing. At n=50 proteins no scheme gives an absolute AUC precise to 0.03, and a 0.01 effect is not measurable here at all, paired or otherwise.

The case for the change is that the current design is not merely imprecise, it is *biased*, and it has no paired-comparison capability. Precision can be bought later with more proteins; bias cannot.

This is a breaking change for the published results. They are computed a different way and will not be reproducible from the new pipeline. Section 9 says what must be preserved so they remain checkable from the archived artefacts.

### 2.2 The current estimators, and how they leak

**Blind test (`thoipapy/validation/testset_trainset.py`).** Fit on `set08`, predict `set07`. It reports `auc(mean_fpr, mean_tpr)` at `:223-231`, the AUC of the **vertically averaged ROC curve**, which is a different estimator from the mean of per-protein AUCs. `leave_one_out.py:251-263` computes both, named `mean_roc_auc_from_joined_data` and `mean_roc_auc_all_prot`. Section 9 requires recording which of the two the paper quoted. Clean in principle, useless in practice (section 2.3).

**Leave-one-out (`thoipapy/validation/leave_one_out.py`).** Refits per protein, correctly excluding pairwise-identity cluster mates (`:146-172`). But what it refits comes from `03_train_data_after_first_feature_seln.csv` and `04_tuned_ensemble_parameters.csv`, both built over all of `set08` including the held-out protein. The refit is honest; the pipeline that produced what it refits is not.

**Ten-fold (`thoipapy/validation/tenfold.py`).** Already `StratifiedGroupKFold` grouped by `acc_db`, stratified on the raw label, `n_splits=10`, reusing the globally-selected features and globally-tuned parameters. Marked NOT MAINTAINED, off in both shipped settings files.

Seven selection steps leak, all running once over the entire training table:

| # | step | module | what it sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | initial hyperparameter tuning | `ML_model/tune.py` via `feature_importance/mean_decrease_impurity.py:13` | all labels, `GridSearchCV(cv=5)` ungrouped |
| 2 | MDI feature ranking | `feature_importance/mean_decrease_impurity.py:24` | all labels |
| 3 | correlated-duplicate removal | `feature_importance/remove_duplicates.py:52` | fully supervised, see below |
| 4 | ANOVA `SelectKBest` | `feature_importance/anova.py` | all labels |
| 5 | ensemble RFE | `feature_importance/ensemble_rfe.py` | all labels |
| 6 | final hyperparameter tuning | `ML_model/tune.py:11` | all labels, `GridSearchCV(cv=5)` ungrouped |
| 7 | feature-importance AUC | `validation/auc.py:24`, called from `feature_importance/mean_decrease_accuracy.py:12` | all labels, plain `StratifiedKFold(10)`, no groups at all |

Row 7 is the estimator behind the published feature-importance ranking (`FigS17_BZ13_feature_importance.png`), which is exactly the kind of number this change exists to make honest.

Row 3 is not a tie-break. `remove_duplicates.py:52-54` drops whichever member of a correlated pair (`abs(r) > 0.6`) has the lower MDI, overridden only by the `features_to_be_retained_during_selection` whitelist. It takes 102 columns down to 44. It is a fully supervised selection step.

Both `GridSearchCV` and `RandomizedSearchCV` are called with integer `cv=5` (`tune.py:84`, `:124`), which is plain `StratifiedKFold` over residues with no groups, so residues of the same TMD land on both sides of the inner split, and so do residues of homologous TMDs.

**Verified as not leaking.** The `interface` label is a plain rename of a pre-existing column: `bind` for crystal/NMR (`add_experimental_data_to_train_set.py:92`), `Interface` for ETRA (`:85`). The rule behind it is the per-protein top-`inter_pair_max` interacting-pair selection (`inter_pair_max = 6`), applied per protein. Note that the 2-10 angstrom and -0.4 to 0.4 constants at `:121-130` produce `interface_score_norm`, a feature-side rescaling, **not** the label: across the 229 `*.6pairmax.bind.closedist.csv` files, 52 contain a negative residue closer than the furthest positive, the furthest positive is 26.38 A and the closest negative 2.19 A, so no distance threshold reproduces `bind`. For ETRA the effective cut varies per protein from 0.243 to 0.552.

No cross-protein quantity enters the label. Three consequences follow:

- The positive count per protein is fixed by construction, which constrains what stratification can achieve and affects how AUBOC should be read.
- The database effect is partly a labelling-rule artefact, which strengthens the case for stratifying on database.
- If `bind_column` is ever switched to `interface_score_norm` (the code branches on it at `leave_one_out.py:342`), those thresholds become the real ones.

Per-residue feature construction is genuinely per protein: `features/normalise_features.py:7` takes a single protein's frame, and `combine_features.py:270` only stacks and drops rows.

### 2.3 The blind test set cannot resolve anything

Fit `ExtraTreesClassifier(n_estimators=200, min_samples_leaf=4, max_features="sqrt", bootstrap=False, random_state=0)` on the 26 selected features of `set08`, predict `set07`:

```
set07 blind test: n_res=194  n_pos=59  n_prot=10   AUC=0.649
  95% CI, resampling residues : 0.554 - 0.737   (width 0.184)
  95% CI, resampling proteins : 0.527 - 0.750   (width 0.223)
  per-protein AUC: [0.34, 0.35, 0.43, 0.67, 0.68, 0.70, 0.70, 0.71, 0.71, 0.91]
  mean 0.620   sd 0.186   sem 0.059
```

The protein-level bootstrap is the honest one, since residues within a TMD are not independent draws. A 95% interval of 0.53 to 0.75 cannot distinguish the current model from a coin flip at one end or from a good predictor at the other. With the actual shipped classifier (`n_estimators=50, criterion=entropy, max_depth=7, min_samples_leaf=5`) the AUC is 0.640 and the protein bootstrap is wider still at 0.263.

### 2.4 How much the leakage is worth

`set05`, k=5, `shuffle=True`, mean over seeds 0 to 9 with the seed-to-seed standard deviation in brackets, features fixed to the 26 chosen on `set08`. That reuse is itself an instance of the leakage under discussion, so all five rows are optimistic by the same unknown amount; only the differences between them are meaningful.

| splitting scheme | pooled OOF AUC | AP |
|---|---|---|
| `StratifiedKFold`, residues shuffled, no grouping | 0.646 (0.009) | 0.459 (0.010) |
| `GroupKFold` by protein | 0.613 (0.008) | 0.421 (0.009) |
| `StratifiedGroupKFold` by protein, strata = database x label | 0.629 (0.008) | 0.433 (0.008) |
| `StratifiedGroupKFold` by homology cluster, strata = database x label | 0.611 (0.003) | 0.425 (0.003) |
| `StratifiedGroupKFold` by homology cluster, strata = database only | 0.613 (0.009) | 0.422 (0.006) |

Shuffling residues across protein boundaries buys **+0.033 AUC** of nothing. Grouping by protein but not by homology buys a further **+0.018**. Together that exceeds the effect size anyone is trying to measure.

The small standard deviation on row 4 (0.003) is not stability. It is the six-level stratum collapsing the space of reachable partitions; see section 3.5.

### 2.5 What the proposed design's own error bar is

Proposed scheme (`StratifiedGroupKFold(5, shuffle=True)`, groups = 43 clusters, strata = database, seed 42, 26 features, cluster bootstrap over 4000 resamples):

```
arm A (26 features)                        AUC 0.6227   marginal 95% CI 0.562 - 0.687   width 0.125
arm B (25 features, minus rate4site4mean)  AUC 0.6184
paired difference A - B                    +0.0043     95% CI -0.0074 to +0.0156       width 0.023
```

The marginal interval is 1.8x narrower than the blind test's 0.223. The **paired** interval is 5.4x narrower again, and is the only one of the three that can resolve a realistic feature effect.

**Acceptance criterion.** The change succeeds if, at the chosen k and `n_repeats`, the paired-difference 95% interval between two configurations differing by one feature is no wider than 0.03. Measured above at 0.023, so the design clears it. Re-check at the end of migration stage 2, before anything is deleted.

## 3. Design decisions

### 3.1 One pool, no held-out set

Use all 50 proteins of `set05`. Delete the `set07` / `set08` split as an evaluation device.

The argument for keeping a blind set is that cross-validation with in-fold model selection still leaks through the choice of procedure. That is real, but a 10-protein test set does not answer it either, because the same person can read the blind-test number fifty times. The defence is a pre-registered short list of variants and honest reporting of how many were tried (section 10), not a test set too small to reject anything.

Ten extra proteins is a 25% increase in training data and a 5.4x increase in evaluation data over the current blind test.

### 3.2 Groups = pairwise-identity clusters, with a documented caveat

Group on the `reduced_clusters` from `<set>_sim_matrix.xlsx`, mapping every protein to an integer cluster id. This is what LOO already does.

Do not fall back to ungrouped splitting when the matrix is missing. Raise. A missing group label silently turns one cluster into five independent singletons, which is precisely the leak the grouping exists to prevent. The group-label lookup must raise on a missing column, duplicate ids, unmapped ids, and the string `"nan"`.

**The caveat.** Identity is computed over the gapped global alignment of full-length sequences running from 36 to 3033 residues, so it is dominated by terminal gap padding and systematically deflated for length-mismatched pairs. The model only ever sees the TMD. Re-aligning the 50 TMD sequences finds **49 cross-cluster pairs at >= 40% TMD identity**, including P05067-NMR / P02724-ETRA at 60.0% and 5irzD6-crystal / P09619-NMR at 48.0%, while *inside* the five-member cluster P09619 and P22607 share only **12.0%** TMD identity.

The grouping therefore over-constrains (locking 3 of 8 NMR proteins into one fold on the basis of homology outside the modelled region) and under-constrains (leaving near-identical TMDs in different folds) at the same time. TMD-level clustering would be *more* conservative for the pairs that matter, not less.

**Recommendation.** Cluster on the TMD plus `num_of_sur_residues` window that the feature pipeline actually consumes, take the union of that grouping with the full-length one, and record both thresholds and the resulting group count in `splits.json` provenance. Run the threshold sweep from section 2.1 and state that 43 is a knife-edge number. This is a stage-1 task.

### 3.3 Strata = database only

Build the stratification vector from `database` alone, three levels.

A six-level `database x label` stratum interacts with the fact that only 6 clusters carry NMR proteins, and collapses the reachable partition space: 50 seeds produce **15 distinct partitions** under `database x label` and **50 out of 50** under `database` alone. The cost of dropping the label is about 0.002 AUC, well inside the seed-to-seed spread of 0.008 (section 2.4, rows 4 and 5). The label contributes nothing to the estimate and destroys the repeats.

Stratifying on `database` is the part that matters. Without it, folds come out with 0 and with 114 NMR residues and no per-fold or per-database statement means anything. The fixed per-protein positive count (section 2.2) means the class balance largely follows the database composition anyway.

Note what is and is not true about the primary metric. Pooled OOF AUC is **not** insensitive to the partition: it is assembled from predictions produced by k different models, so it depends on the partition through the training sets and on how comparable the k models' score scales are. Changing the stratum definition moves the headline number by roughly 0.017. So the stratum is a design choice with a measurable effect, not a free one. Fix it once and pre-register it, as section 10 already requires for `master_seed`.

### 3.4 k = 5, and 5 is the maximum

Measured fold composition, `StratifiedGroupKFold` on the homology clusters:

`set05`, k=5: proteins per fold `[11, 9, 11, 10, 9]`, all three databases present in every fold, positives per fold in the range 49 to 75 depending on seed.

`set05`, k=10: proteins per fold `[8, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 5, 6, 4]`, **4 of 10 folds contain zero NMR residues**.

`set08`, k=10: proteins per fold `[7, 4, 4, 3, 4, 4, 3, 2, 5, 4]`, **6 of 10 folds contain zero NMR residues**, smallest fold 2 proteins / 48 residues / 11 positives.

**The hard ceiling is 6.** Distinct groups per stratum on `set05` are `{crystal: 20, ETRA: 18, NMR: 6}`: the 8 NMR proteins occupy only 6 clusters, because three of them are inside the five-member cluster. The guard in section 5.3 counts groups per stratum, so it caps k at 6 and k=5 has a margin of one. If the clustering threshold ever moves to 14% the NMR count can fall below 5 and the guard will reject k=5 as well.

**k=5 is already infeasible on `set08`**, where the NMR stratum spans only 4 clusters. Anyone trying the new scheme on the old training set gets a raise. Migration step 3 accounts for this.

The cost of k=5 versus k=10 is that each model trains on 80% rather than 90% of the pool, so the estimate is slightly pessimistic relative to a model trained on everything. That is the correct direction to err.

### 3.5 Repeats: `shuffle=True` is mandatory

Set `n_repeats = 3`, seeds `master_seed + 1000 * repeat`.

**`shuffle=True` must be passed explicitly.** `StratifiedGroupKFold` silently ignores `random_state` unless `shuffle=True`: three different seeds without it produce one partition. This fails invisibly and defeats every guard in section 5.3, because three copies of the same partition still test every residue exactly three times and the coverage invariant passes cleanly. The repeats become decorative and nothing notices.

With `shuffle=True` and `database`-only strata, 50 seeds give 50 distinct partitions, so repeats are genuine replicates. Under `database x label` strata they are not: at `master_seed = 42`, the partitions for seeds 1042 and 2042 are identical, so `n_repeats = 3` would buy 2 partitions and burn a third of the compute reproducing a number bit for bit.

**Guard.** Draw seeds until `n_repeats` distinct partitions have been found, deduplicating on the canonical frozenset-of-frozensets form, record the seeds actually used in `splits.json`, and raise if fewer than `n_repeats` distinct partitions exist. The determinism test must assert the seed-to-partition mapping, not merely that `splits.json` is byte-reproducible: a file that reproduces the same wrong thing every time passes that test.

### 3.6 Reporting: five estimators, and the paired one carries the inference

1. **Paired group bootstrap of the difference between two configurations on byte-identical folds. This is the primary inferential claim.** Resample the 43 clusters with replacement, recompute both arms' pooled AUC on the resample, take percentiles of the difference. Width 0.023 measured (section 2.5). Every reported comparison goes through this.
2. **Pooled OOF ROC AUC and average precision, per repeat.** The descriptive level. Every residue has exactly one prediction per repeat, from a model that saw neither its protein nor its cluster. Describe it as *the performance of the procedure on a residue drawn from this pool*, not as what a single deployed model does: it is one AUC over scores emitted by k differently-fitted models with k different feature sets, so it is sensitive to between-fold score-scale differences and the bias is generally downward. Report the mean-of-fold AUC alongside as a calibration-insensitive cross-check.
3. **Mean per-protein AUC, with the per-protein values listed.** Rank-based within protein and therefore immune to the score-scale problem in item 2, which is its real virtue.
4. **AUBOC from the existing BO-curve machinery.** See section 3.8 for why this is not free.
5. **Marginal cluster bootstrap on the pooled OOF AUC.** Width about 0.125. Report it as uncertainty on the level, *conditional on these folds*. It holds the training folds fixed and captures evaluation-sample variability only, not the larger term from re-drawing the training set; a resampled cluster appearing twice contributes duplicated predictions from a model that saw the other 42, which makes it anti-conservative. It is the best cheap marginal interval available and a real improvement on the across-fold standard deviation, which is dominated by fold size and moves with k for reasons unrelated to the model. Bengio and Grandvalet (2004) is the reference for there being no unbiased estimator of k-fold CV variance.

Do **not** use the Nadeau and Bengio (2003) corrected resampled t-test here. It assumes n independent paired samples, and with 15 splits drawn from 3 partitions the corrected variance would still be computed as if n = 15. The paired cluster bootstrap makes no such assumption.

Report metrics sliced by database. With 8 NMR proteins in 6 clusters the NMR slice will be very wide; say so rather than omitting it. Even with database stratification one fold holds 71 of 180 NMR residues (39%), because the five-member cluster is indivisible, so per-database NMR statements stay fragile.

### 3.7 Splits as a versioned artefact

Generate the folds once, write them to `data/results/<set>/crossvalidation/splits.json`, and have every downstream step read that file. Contents: scheme, `n_folds`, `n_repeats`, `master_seed`, the seeds actually used, `shuffle`, the id column, the group column and cluster source path and threshold, the stratum definition, `fold_assignments` mapping `split_id -> {repeat, fold}`, per-split explicit `train` and `test` id lists, a coverage summary, and provenance (git SHA, dirty flag, timestamp, package versions).

This is what makes estimator 1 in section 3.6 possible at all. Without it, "config B beat config A" may be "config B got an easier split".

`data/results/` is DVC-tracked. Decide explicitly that `splits.json`, the per-split prediction CSVs and the metrics JSON are tracked, since the whole purpose is that `splits.json` is a comparable object across runs.

### 3.8 The residue population, and why the BO curve is not free

**Mechanically.** `validation/bocurve.py:6` requires an `interface_score` column in the same frame as the prediction column, and `drop_cols_not_used_in_ML` strips it. The per-database sign flip currently lives at `leave_one_out.py:370-372`, inside the function being deleted. The orchestrator must carry `interface_score` alongside `X` and reimplement the flip.

**Substantively.** With `remove_crystal_hetero = TRUE` (the shipped default), `combine_features.py:306` builds the training table from the `.nohetero` variant for crystal proteins. LOO by contrast *tests* on the full per-protein file **including** hetero-contact residues (`leave_one_out.py:66-68`, `:183`), and that asymmetry is deliberate and documented. An OOF pipeline that predicts the rows of the training table therefore evaluates on a different residue population, from which the hardest crystal negatives have been removed, which moves the number upward.

**Decision.** Predict on the **full per-protein combined file**, matching LOO. This preserves the AUBOC comparison to the paper, keeps `interface_score` available for the BO curve, and avoids confounding the migration comparison. The training half stays hetero-removed, as today.

## 4. Order of operations inside a fold

Everything below the line "per fold" is fitted on `X_train` and only applied to `X_test`.

```
filter the pool first:  min_n_homol_training, dropna_with_report
generate splits from (ids, homology_group, database), shuffle=True, write splits.json
assert the id list in splits.json equals the filtered pool

per outer fold (5 folds x 3 repeats = 15):
    rows_train <- residues whose protein is not in this fold
    rows_test  <- residues whose protein is in this fold

    # -- COLUMN FILTER, FIRST, BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE --
    X = drop_cols_not_used_in_ML(...)        # 115 cols -> 102 eligible
    assert bind_column not in X.columns
    assert "interface_score" not in X.columns
    assert "interface_score_norm" not in X.columns

    # -- unsupervised, train-fitted --
    drop zero-variance columns                        fit: X_train
    correlated-duplicate filter (r > 0.6)             fit: X_train
        decided by MDI computed on X_train, y_train   <- supervised, see open q. 5
    optional PCA over an embedding block              fit: X_train        <- future work, sec 8
    optional scaler                                   fit: X_train

    # -- supervised, train-fitted --
    MDI ranking                                       fit: X_train, y_train
    ANOVA SelectKBest                                 fit: X_train, y_train
    ensemble RFE                                      fit: X_train, y_train
    merge anova + rfe + always-retained               -> feature list for THIS fold

    # -- tuning, train-fitted, inner grouped CV --
    inner CV = StratifiedGroupKFold(n_splits=3, shuffle=True,
                 groups=homology_group[train], strata=database[train])
    search over the reduced feature matrix            fit: X_train, y_train

    # -- final fit, then predict on the FULL per-protein files (sec 3.8) --
    fit ExtraTreesClassifier(best_params) on X_train[fold_features], y_train
    for each test protein: read combined_features_csv (hetero INCLUDED),
        reindex to fold_features, predict_proba
    record: split_id, repeat, fold, sample_id, acc_db, database, interface_score,
            y_true, y_pred, selected features, chosen hyperparameters, fit time
```

**The column filter is not optional and not a detail.** `01_train_data_orig.csv` has 115 columns and only 102 are `include=TRUE` in `thoipapy/setting/model_features.csv`. Today the filter runs exactly once, in `validation/feature_selection.py:7`, before `03_train_data_after_first_feature_seln.csv` is written. This design retires that file as the entry point, so the filter has to be re-established explicitly at the top of the fold. Taking "all numeric columns except the obvious label columns" instead leaves eight ineligible features in place, including `interface_score_norm`, which correlates 0.679 with the label:

```
cols in the naive matrix but not include=TRUE:
  Hydrophobicity_sAA, PI_sAA, Aromatic_sAA, Polar_sAA, Small_sAA,
  LIPS_entropy, res_num_full_seq, interface_score_norm

(k=5, shuffle=True, seed 0, strata = database, groups = the 43 clusters)
pooled OOF AUC, 26 eligible features : 0.618
pooled OOF AUC, naive all-numeric    : 0.931
```

The assertions in section 5.3 exist so this cannot be reintroduced silently.

Other notes on the ordering:

- **Feature selection precedes tuning.** The reverse order tunes `max_features` against a 102-column matrix and applies the chosen fraction to a 26-column one, which is a different regularisation strength than the one searched.
- **The inner CV objective remains slightly optimistic**, because features are selected on the whole training fold including the rows that then act as inner validation. Fully nesting the selection inside the inner loop would multiply cost by another factor of 3 and is not worth it here. The outer estimate is unaffected; only the tuning criterion is contaminated. Say this in the README.
- **Inner k=3, grouped, `shuffle=True`.** A training fold holds about 34 clusters. Guard on distinct groups per stratum within the training fold.
- **`groups=None` must never fall through.** scikit-learn silently ignores `groups` when `cv` is an integer, which is exactly the bug in today's `tune.py`. Raise if a tuning config is supplied without groups.

**Accepted residual leak, documented rather than fixed.** Every conservation and coevolution feature comes from a BLAST alignment of the query TMD plus 20 residues, filtered at `min_identity_of_TMD_seq = 0.2`. Nothing prevents a test-fold protein from appearing as a homologue in a training protein's alignment, and given the 49 cross-cluster pairs at >= 40% TMD identity (section 3.2) that is not hypothetical. It leaks feature space, not labels, and cannot be fixed short of rebuilding every MSA per fold. Name it in the README as an accepted residual.

The rule about reuse between folds is specifically: **no fitted transformer, selection or hyperparameter set is ever reused between folds.** The feature table, the PSSMs, the rate4site outputs and the similarity matrix are all built once over the whole pool and the pipeline cannot do otherwise.

The selected features and chosen hyperparameters differ between folds. That is correct and it is the point. It also means "which features does THOIPA use" has two answers: the per-fold ones, which the estimate is honest about, and the shipped ones, from running the identical procedure once on all 50 proteins (section 7).

#### 5.1.1 What `run_validation` becomes

The stage currently consumes artefacts produced only by the modules being deleted. The replacement must specify:

- The predictor column name that replaces `THOIPA_{set_number}_LOO`. Proposal: `THOIPA_{set_number}_OOF`.
- Where per-protein OOF prediction CSVs are written, so `combine_mult_predictors` can find them. Proposal: `predictions/THOIPA_OOF/{database}.{acc}.OOF.prediction.csv`, mirroring the LOO layout exactly so the consumers need a name change and nothing else.
- Whether `merge_predictions`, `roc` and `precision_recall` lose their trainset/testset arguments (recommended) or keep them pointing at `set05`.

### 5.2 Settings changes

Remove `test_datasets`, `train_datasets` and `run_testset_trainset_validation` from **both** shipped settings files.

Change `set_number` from 8 to 5, and `cross_validation_number_of_splits` from 10 to 5.

Add:

| key | value | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `cross_validation_n_repeats` | 3 | see 3.5 |
| `cross_validation_master_seed` | 42 | |
| `cross_validation_stratify_on` | `database` | see 3.3 |
| `cross_validation_inner_n_splits` | 3 | inner CV for tuning |
| `tune_hyperparameters_in_fold` | TRUE in `run_settings_example.csv`, **FALSE in `standalone_run_settings.csv`** | the FALSE default is what lets the functional test pass, see 5.4 |

Two mechanical requirements that are code changes with their own tests, not config edits:

- `thoipapy/common.py:183` builds a plain dict and `run_one_set` reads `s["..."]` directly, so a settings file written before this change raises `KeyError` mid-run. Every new key needs either a default in `common.py` or a load-time completeness check.
- Rejecting retired keys contradicts `test/unit/test_run_settings.py:62`. `RunSettings.from_settings` deliberately ignores unknown keys because the settings dict carries much more than stage flags. Either scope the rejection to a known list of retired keys, or change the test and say why.

### 5.3 Guards, all raising rather than warning

- **Distinct groups per stratum >= n_folds.** Count groups, not rows. Counting rows is not merely weaker, it is the wrong check: 100 rows in 4 groups across 2 strata clears any row-based guard, `StratifiedGroupKFold` then packs every group into the first four folds, and the remaining folds come back with empty test sets that nothing downstream notices. scikit-learn only raises when *every* stratum is too small, so this is deliberately stricter than the splitter. On `set05` this guard caps k at 6 and rejects k=5 on `set08`.
- **`shuffle=True` is set, and the `n_repeats` partitions are pairwise distinct.** Without this the coverage invariant passes on three identical partitions.
- **No column that derives from the label is in `X`.** Assert `bind_column`, `interface_score` and `interface_score_norm` are absent before the first fit.
- **Coverage: every residue tested exactly `n_repeats` times.** Raise, do not warn.
- **The id list in `splits.json` equals the filtered pool.** Row filters (`min_n_homol_training`, `dropna_with_report`) run *before* split generation; if they run after, the coverage invariant fails on rows that no longer exist.
- **No protein appears in both train and test of the same split.**
- **Similarity matrix present and every protein mapped.** No silent fallback to `acc_db`.
- **`groups` supplied wherever a `cv` argument is.** Never pass an integer `cv` to a search object.
- **No empty test fold.** Unreachable given the first guard; keep it anyway.

### 5.4 Determinism

Keep the existing `RANDOM_STATE = 0` and the two order-dependence fixes already in the codebase (`drop_cols_not_used_in_ML` column ordering, `merge_top_features_anova_ensemble` deduplication). Add:

- Split generation seeded only from `cross_validation_master_seed`, `master_seed + 1000 * repeat`, `shuffle=True`, with the distinctness search of section 3.5 and the seeds actually used recorded in `splits.json`.
- Per-fold tuning search seeded as `master_seed + split_index`, so folds do not run correlated searches from the same constant.
- The search seed is never reused as the classifier's `random_state`; otherwise changing how the search is seeded silently reseeds every fitted forest.
- Bootstrap seeded explicitly.
- A unit test asserting the seed-to-partition mapping, not merely that `splits.json` is byte-reproducible.

**The functional test.** `test/functional/test_ml_pipeline.py:51` already sets `run_validation = False`, so the outer CV never runs there. What breaks is the *inner* grouped CV, which runs under `run_feature_selection` (line 46) and `train_machine_learning_model` (line 49), both switched on, against a 2-protein set that yields at most 2 clusters against `cross_validation_inner_n_splits = 3`. The fix is `tune_hyperparameters_in_fold = FALSE` in `standalone_run_settings.csv` with the test relying on it, or enlarging the functional protein set. Do not weaken the guard.

## 6. Cost

The grid is 2 criteria x 6 `n_estimators` x 2 `max_features` x 6 `max_depth` x 4 `min_samples_leaf` x 1 `bootstrap` = **576 combinations** (`tune.py:60-83`), plus `RandomizedSearchCV(n_iter=100, cv=5)` at `:121`. Both run twice per pipeline pass.

Measured on 1044 rows x 102 columns: 0.046 s per fit single-threaded at `n_estimators=50`, 0.183 s at 200; 0.123 s at `n_jobs=-1`. The in-fold grid is 15 x 576 x 3 = 25,920 fits, which is roughly 52 minutes serial and single-digit minutes on 16 cores, on the 26-to-44 column reduced matrix rather than the 102-column one.

Cost is therefore not a reason to trim anything. The recommendations that stand on their own merits:

- **Drop `RandomizedSearchCV` from the in-fold path.** Its output was already documented as advisory and never fed the model. Keep it as a one-off exploration on the whole pool, labelled as not part of the estimate.
- **Provide `tune_hyperparameters_in_fold = FALSE`.** For iterating on features, fixed hyperparameters plus in-fold feature selection is fast and still honest. This is also what makes the functional test pass (section 5.4).
- **Watch `n_jobs=-1` nesting.** Setting it on both the estimator and the search oversubscribes the machine.

## 7. The shipped predictor

`thoipapy/ML_model/THOIPA_trained_ML_model.lpkl` plus `thoipapy/ML_model/model_features.txt` (26 features) are what `predict.py:99,306` loads. Both are committed to git and the webserver depends on them.

Cross-validation estimates the performance of a *procedure*. It does not produce a deployable model. The shipped artefacts come from running the identical procedure once with the training fold set to all 50 proteins:

```
final_features, final_params = run_selection_and_tuning(X_all, y_all,
                                                        groups=homology_group,
                                                        strata=database)
model = ExtraTreesClassifier(**final_params, random_state=0).fit(X_all[final_features], y_all)
```

`run_selection_and_tuning` must be literally the same function the folds call, with the same inner grouped CV, so the estimate describes the thing that was shipped. Write `model_features.txt` from `final_features` in the same step that dumps the pickle.

**Column order is load-bearing.** `predict.py:306-309` does `df_data[model_features]`, so the order of the 26 lines must match the column order the pickled model was fitted with. `test/unit/test_determinism.py` exists precisely because `ExtraTreesClassifier` draws its `max_features` candidates by column index. Assert `list(final_features) == list(fit.feature_names_in_)` at write time.

The shipped feature list and hyperparameters will change, because the pool grows from 40 to 50 proteins and the inner CV becomes grouped. The webserver's predictions will move.

## 8. What this unlocks

Each of these is currently blocked by the leakage, not by the code.

- **Embedding features (ESM-2, ESM-C, ProtT5 per-residue).** Hundreds to thousands of columns against 1044 rows. Unusable without in-fold dimensionality reduction. With `fold_pipeline` owning the transform fits, a per-block PCA (fit `StandardScaler` plus `PCA` per named feature group on the training fold only) drops straight in.
- **Real hyperparameter tuning.** Currently pointless: the tuned parameters are chosen with the evaluation data's labels in view.
- **Honest feature ablations.** "Does rate4site help?" is answerable once the answer is not partly "rate4site helped choose the features". This is the comparison the paired instrument in section 2.5 was demonstrated on.
- **Alternative estimators.** Gradient boosting, logistic regression on embeddings, per-database calibration.

## 10. Guarding against procedure-level overfitting

Since there is no held-out set, the discipline has to be explicit.

- Write down the variants to be tried **before** running them, in a file in the repository, with the date.
- Report the count of variants tried alongside the best result. "Best of 12" is a different claim from "the one thing we tried".
- Fix `master_seed` once and never tune it. Fix `cross_validation_stratify_on` once and never tune it: it moves the headline number by about 0.017.
- Every reported comparison uses the same `splits.json` for both arms and the paired cluster bootstrap of section 3.6 item 1.
- Re-run the final chosen configuration under a fresh `master_seed` and report both. If the two disagree materially, the difference was noise.

## 11. Open questions

1. **The clustering threshold and level (section 3.2).** 15% over gapped full-length alignments is a knife edge (43 groups at 15%, 36 at 14%) and does not track TMD identity, which is what the model sees. Is the union of full-length and TMD-level clustering the right answer, or does TMD-level alone suffice, and at what cutoff? Highest-value question.
2. **Is `set05` really the whole pool?** Were proteins excluded during curation for train/test balance reasons rather than data quality, and can they be readmitted? Ten more proteins would do more for precision than anything else in this document.
3. **Per-database reporting at 6 NMR clusters.** Is an NMR-sliced metric meaningful when one fold holds 39% of all NMR residues? Or should NMR and crystal merge into a single "structure" stratum for fold construction while still being reported separately?
4. **`min_n_homol_training` units.** `combine_features.py:200` stores `n_homologues = np.cbrt(n_uniq_TMD_seqs)`, and three call sites filter `n_homologues >= min_n_homol_training`. A threshold of 20 would drop every row. It is 0 in both shipped settings so it is inert, but the unit mismatch should be fixed or the setting renamed while this area is open.
5. **The duplicate filter.** It is fully supervised (section 2.2), so it belongs in the fold. If a fold-varying feature space proves too noisy, the fallback is to break correlated pairs on **variance** rather than MDI, which makes the step genuinely unsupervised and safe to fit once over the whole pool.
6. **A pre-existing LOO bug, if LOO survives stage 3.** `leave_one_out.py:197-200` continues past a protein present in `df_set` but absent from the training data without appending to `val_list`, and `:229-233` then re-derives `acc_db` by positional index into `df_set.acc_db`. One skipped protein misaligns every subsequent per-protein AUC. It does not fire on the current sets but will corrupt any comparison run on a filtered pool.
